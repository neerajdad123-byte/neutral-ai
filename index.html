Prompt-side bias reduction

Every detail in a prompt is an instruction.

A model reads a name, a job title, a word like boyfriend, and fills in the rest. Some of what it fills in changes the answer. Neutral sits between your people and the model, removes the detail the question does not need, and hands the answer back in their own words.

Method

Three ways a prompt carries what it should not.

Only the first one is visible to the person writing it. The other two are why dropping names is not enough on its own.

Stated

JabariEmblamy boyfriendI'm the CEO

A name, a job title, a relationship word. The trait is in the words, and the model has no reason to treat any of it as anything other than true.

Correlated

CEO → malenurse → femaleEmbla → Norwegian

Nobody wrote a gender. But a role carries the base rate the model learned for it, and that rate walks into the answer. A trait that was never stated still arrives.

Structural

one essay, sent for grading

One essay submitted for a grade reads as the writer's own, so the model answers the way a teacher answers a student: warm, encouraging, slow to reach for the hard note. No single word caused it. The shape of the request did.

Neutral treats all three as input it is allowed to change, and it looks for the second and third hardest, because those are the ones the person typing cannot see.

Pipeline

What happens between the prompt and the model.

One request in, one request out, with a decision made per span rather than over the prompt as a whole.

Tagging

Mark every span, and what it implies

Names, pronouns, roles, relationship words, and the traits each one carries at base rates. A span is marked for what it says and for what it lets the model infer.

Necessity

One question per span

Does the task get worse without it? A role that is the subject of the task stays. A role mentioned in passing goes. The check runs per span, so one prompt can get both answers.

Repair

Remove, or offset

Removal when the span does nothing. Counter-balance when it does something and cannot be dropped, which means adding information that offsets the inference. Every counter-balance is logged as a cost, not a win.

The pairing constraint

The order you name two people in is a signal of its own.

Take the names out and the model goes looking for another way to work out which of the two is writing. The cheapest one available is sentence order.

If every sentence puts Person A first, the model reads Person A as the writer, and then it answers the writer's side of the question more kindly. That is sycophancy arriving through grammar. Neutral rewrites the prompt as descriptive pairs and balances who leads.

Balanced

When the count is odd, Neutral allows a difference of one and picks the direction at random. It never emits a prompt where one person leads every pair. The sentences still say the same things; only the order changed.

Return pass

The answer comes back in the reader's words.

The model answered a prompt that no longer had your people's names in it, so Neutral puts them back, and fixes the pronouns the model chose on its own.

The five-essay case

The model read five essays and graded them in order. Neutral keeps the passage about your essay, drops the other four, and puts the model's third-person reading of the writer into the second person. The original sits behind the button to the left, unchanged.

Limits

What Neutral does not do.

Removing information is a real intervention with real costs, and a vendor who will not name them is telling you something about the rest of the pitch.

It does not make a model unbiased

It removes one class of input that bias travels in. Whatever the model learned from its training is still there, waiting for a prompt that gives it something to work with.

Counter-balancing adds information

Four extra essays so the model cannot tell which one is yours also means four more essays for it to read, and four more chances for it to weight something you did not intend. Neutral takes that route only when removal is unavailable, and records it.

It only sees the prompt

Bias entering through a retrieval corpus, a fine-tune, or a tool's output is outside its reach. Neutral reads the request and the answer, and nothing else.

Sometimes the detail was load-bearing

Drop a role the task actually turns on and the answer gets worse, not fairer. This is why the necessity check runs span by span, and why a pilot measures answer quality alongside bias.

Pilot

Run it on your own prompts.

Two weeks, one team, one task. You choose the task, and we send you every prompt Neutral changed, the change it made, and the two answers side by side.

You give us

A sample of prompts from one team, and the model you already route them to.

You get back

Every changed prompt with its diff, both answers, and the counter-balance log.

The test

Blind-graded answer quality next to the unmodified runs. If the answers are not better, the pilot says so.
