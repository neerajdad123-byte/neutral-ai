01 — Tag

Mark every span, and what it implies.

Names, pronouns, roles, relationship words, and the traits each one carries at base rates. A span gets marked for what it says and for what it lets the model infer. The second list is longer than the first, and it is the one that matters.

02 — Necessity

Does the task get worse without it?

A role that is the subject of the task stays. A role mentioned in passing goes. The check runs span by span, so one prompt can get both answers at once, and the reasons are recorded either way.

03 — Repair

Remove it, or offset it.

Removal when the span does nothing. Counter-balance when it does something and cannot be dropped, which means adding information that offsets the inference. Every counter-balance is logged as a cost, not as a win.

The pairing constraint

The order you name two people in is a signal of its own.

Balanced

When the count is odd, Neutral allows a difference of one and picks the direction at random. It never emits a prompt where one person leads every pair. The sentences still say the same things; only the order changed.

Return pass

The answer comes back in the reader's words.

The model answered a prompt that no longer had your people's names in it, so Neutral puts them back, and fixes the pronouns the model chose on its own.

The five-essay case

The model read five essays and graded them in order. Neutral keeps the passage about your essay, drops the other four, and puts the model's third-person reading of the writer into the second person. The original sits behind the button above, unchanged.

Where it leaks

Three ways a prompt carries what it should not.

Only the first is visible to the person writing it. The other two are why dropping names is not enough on its own.

01 — Stated

Stated

JabariEmblamy boyfriendI'm the CEO

A name, a job title, a relationship word. The trait is in the words, and the model has no reason to treat any of it as anything other than true.

02 — Correlated

Correlated

CEO → malenurse → femaleEmbla → Norwegian

Nobody wrote a gender. But a role carries the base rate the model learned for it, and that rate walks into the answer. A trait that was never stated still arrives.

03 — Structural

Structural

one essay, sent for grading

One essay submitted for a grade reads as the writer's own, so the model answers the way a teacher answers a student: warm, encouraging, slow to reach for the hard note. No single word caused it. The shape of the request did.

Neutral treats all three as input it is allowed to change, and it works hardest on the second and third, because those are the ones the person typing cannot see.

Specification

What it is, in the terms your security team will ask about.

Placement
In the request path, in front of your model gateway. Hosted, or inside your own tenancy.
Model support
Any text model behind an HTTP API. Neutral never sees the model's weights and does not fine-tune anything.
Latency budget
A 250 ms added-latency target per request for prompts under 4,000 tokens. Measured per deployment, not asserted.
Retention
Prompts and answers are held for the length of the session by default. Longer retention is opt-in and scoped per team.
Visibility
Every rewrite is logged with the span, the decision, and the reason. Nothing is changed silently.
Rollout
Shadow mode first. Neutral rewrites in the background and logs the diff without altering what the model sees, until you turn it on.

Targets, not benchmarks. A pilot measures them on your traffic and reports what it finds.

Limits

What Neutral does not do.

Removing information is a real intervention with real costs, and a vendor who will not name them is telling you something about the rest of the pitch.

It does not make a model unbiased

It removes one class of input that bias travels in. Whatever the model learned from its training is still there, waiting for a prompt that gives it something to work with.

Counter-balancing adds information

Four extra essays so the model cannot tell which one is yours also means four more essays for it to read, and four more chances for it to weight something you did not intend. Neutral takes that route only when removal is unavailable, and records every instance.

It only sees the prompt

Bias entering through a retrieval corpus, a fine-tune, or a tool's output is outside its reach. Neutral reads the request and the answer, and nothing else.

Sometimes the detail was load-bearing

Drop a role the task actually turns on and the answer gets worse, not fairer. This is why the necessity check runs span by span, and why a pilot measures answer quality next to bias rather than instead of it.
